    Frequently Asked Questions

    Why do I get hungry on a diet?

    Hunger during a diet is primarily driven by hormonal changes. When you reduce calorie intake, ghrelin (the "hunger hormone") levels rise while leptin (the "fullness hormone") levels drop. This is your body's natural survival response to an energy deficit. The good news is that this response typically subsides after 2-4 weeks as your body adapts to the new energy balance.

    How can I stop cravings naturally?

    To reduce cravings naturally, focus on eating high-volume, low-calorie foods like vegetables and fruits to stretch stomach receptors and signal fullness. Prioritize protein at every meal (25-40g per meal) to increase satiety through GLP-1 and PYY release. Stay hydrated, get adequate sleep (7-9 hours), and practice mindful eating by removing distractions during meals.

    Does hunger mean my diet isn't working?

    Not at all. Some level of hunger is a normal and expected part of being in a calorie deficit. However, persistent extreme hunger may indicate your deficit is too aggressive or your food choices lack satiety. If you're losing 0.5-1% of body weight per week and feel manageable hunger, your diet is working as intended. Consider adjusting your macro split toward more protein and fiber if hunger becomes unmanageable.
