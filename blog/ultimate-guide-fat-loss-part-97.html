    Frequently Asked Questions

    What formula should I use to calculate my deficit?

    The Mifflin-St Jeor equation is the most accurate for the general population. For men: BMR = 10 x weight(kg) + 6.25 x height(cm) - 5 x age(y) + 5. For women: BMR = 10 x weight(kg) + 6.25 x height(cm) - 5 x age(y) - 161. Multiply by 1.2-1.9 based on activity level for TDEE, then subtract 300-500 calories.

    How accurate are TDEE calculators?

    TDEE calculators provide an estimate within 10-15% of actual values for most people. Individual variation in metabolic rate, NEAT, and TEF means the calculator gives you a starting point, not a perfect number. Track your weight for 2-3 weeks and adjust intake by 100-200 calories based on actual weekly weight trends.

    Should I adjust my deficit as I lose weight?

    Absolutely. As you lose weight, your BMR and TDEE decrease because a smaller body requires fewer calories. Recalculate your deficit every 5-10 pounds lost. A common mistake is keeping the same calorie target throughout, which turns a moderate deficit into a maintenance or surplus over time.
