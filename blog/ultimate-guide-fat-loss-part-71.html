    Frequently Asked Questions

    What counts as NEAT?

    NEAT includes all physical activity that is not deliberate exercise: walking to the car, taking stairs, standing while working, cooking, cleaning, gardening, fidgeting, and even posture changes. Research from Dr. James Levine shows NEAT can vary by up to 2,000 calories per day between sedentary and highly active individuals.

    How many extra steps should I aim for?

    For fat loss, a baseline of 7,000-8,000 steps per day is recommended for general health. To create a meaningful energy deficit through NEAT, aim for 10,000-12,500 steps daily. Increase gradually by 1,000-2,000 steps per week to avoid fatigue. Steps above 15,000 may cut into recovery if not supported by adequate nutrition.

    Can NEAT replace dedicated cardio?

    NEAT should complement, not replace, dedicated cardio. While high NEAT increases total daily calorie burn, it does not provide the cardiovascular conditioning, EPOC effect, or metabolic stimulus that structured cardio offers. The most effective fat loss programs combine high NEAT with 2-3 sessions of intentional cardio per week.
