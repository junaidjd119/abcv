    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is volumetric eating?

    Volumetric eating is a nutrition strategy that prioritizes foods with low calorie density but high volume. By choosing foods rich in water and fiber Ã¢â‚¬â€ such as leafy greens, berries, soups, and lean proteins Ã¢â‚¬â€ you can fill your stomach and trigger satiety signals while consuming fewer calories. This approach makes it easier to maintain a calorie deficit without feeling hungry.

    What foods are best for volume eating?

    The best foods for volume eating include non-starchy vegetables (broccoli, spinach, cauliflower, zucchini), fruits like berries and melons, lean proteins (chicken breast, fish, egg whites), broth-based soups, and whole grains like oatmeal and quinoa. These foods provide high water and fiber content, which stretches the stomach and activates stretch receptors that signal fullness to the brain.

    Can you really eat more and still lose weight?

    Yes. By replacing calorie-dense foods with high-volume, low-calorie alternatives, you can eat the same (or greater) volume of food while consuming significantly fewer calories. For example, swapping 2 tablespoons of oil for a pound of steamed vegetables saves roughly 200 calories while adding more food volume. This makes the diet more sustainable and reduces feelings of deprivation.
