Why BMR Matters for Fat Loss and Muscle Gain

Your BMR determines the minimum calories your body needs. Eating below BMR for extended periods can slow metabolism and cause muscle loss. For fat loss, aim for a deficit between BMR and TDEE. For muscle gain, eat above TDEE while meeting protein targets. Tracking BMR changes over time helps you adjust calories as your body adapts.

WHR Calculator

What Waist-to-Hip Ratio Means

Waist-to-Hip Ratio (WHR) compares the size of your waist to your hips and is a key indicator of fat distribution and health risk. Bodybuilders use WHR to track changes in body composition and abdominal fat over time.

WHR and Health Risk

WHR Ratio

--

Health Risk

--

Health Guidelines: For men: Low risk (<0.95), Moderate risk (0.96-1.0), High risk (>1.0). For women: Low risk (<0.80), Moderate risk (0.81-0.85), High risk (>0.85). Higher ratios indicate increased health risks.

How to Measure WHR Correctly

Measure your waist at the narrowest point (usually just above the navel) while standing and breathing normally. Measure your hips at the widest point around the buttocks. Divide waist by hip measurement for your WHR. Take measurements at the same time of day for consistency.

Frequently Asked Questions

What Is the Difference Between BMR and TDEE?

BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) is the calories your body burns at complete rest to perform basic life functions. TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) is BMR plus all calories burned through movement, exercise, and digestion. Use BMR as your baseline; use TDEE to set calorie targets for weight change.

Is BMI Accurate for Fit People?

BMI does not distinguish between muscle and fat. Athletes and fit individuals with high muscle mass may be classified as overweight despite low body fat. For a more accurate assessment, combine BMI with body fat percentage and LBM measurements.

How Often Should I Recalculate My Macros?

Recalculate your macros whenever your calorie target changes or after losing or gaining 5-10 pounds. Major changes in activity level also warrant a recalculation. For most people, every 4-6 weeks is sufficient to keep your nutrition aligned with your current body composition.

Which Calculator Should I Use for Weight Loss?
